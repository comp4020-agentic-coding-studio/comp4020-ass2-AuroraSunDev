Two members of staff run this course, and they own different things. Sending a
question to the wrong one costs you a day, so the split is written down rather
than left to be worked out.

**Marisol Quaye** convenes the course. She sets the twelve-week testing
programme, owns the four failure families — which she is careful to describe as
a teaching classification rather than a finding — and marks the final
structural defences. Anything about what the course requires, what a criterion
means, or an extension goes to her.

**Idris Fenn** runs the laboratory. He maintains the test stations, issues the
supplied evidence pack, and arranges every alternative participation route.
Anything about access to equipment, a specimen you cannot obtain, or a reason
you would rather not handle food goes to him — and it does not need a
justification attached.

## When to bring work rather than email it

Consultations run in **Week 4** and **Week 9**. They exist for work in progress:
a half-built comparison, a record you are not sure counts as evidence, a
prediction you think is wrong. Both weeks sit immediately before a submission,
which is when a design is still cheap to change.

Email is for questions with short answers. A draft is not a short answer, and
sending one by email usually gets you an invitation to a consultation anyway.

## If something here is wrong

Where a brief, the [Field Manual](/field-manual/) and a week page disagree about
a method, tell either of them. One of the three is wrong and it is not always
the one you are reading.
