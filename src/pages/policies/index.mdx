# Safe participation. Equivalent evidence.

No student is required to prepare, purchase, handle, or consume food to meet a
course learning outcome.

These rules apply to every session, every test station, and every piece of
graded work. Where an assessment brief and this page disagree, this page
governs; tell a member of staff so the brief can be corrected.

## Three equivalent routes

Every practical activity in the course can be completed by any one of three
routes.

## Food handling and consumption

Testing in this course is structural. Eating is not part of it.

- You are never required to eat a specimen.
- Tested food must not be presented to another person for consumption.
- Shared workspaces are cleaned before and after physical testing.
- Cutting activities use supplied pre-cut specimens, diagrams, or supervised
  equipment. This holds in the week built around the cut,
  [Week 9: Cutting Geometry](/sessions/week-09/), as it does everywhere else.
- A structural result never overrides a food-safety requirement.

## Allergies and sensory access

An alternative route is available on request, and the request is the whole
process.

- You do not need to disclose a medical diagnosis in order to select an
  alternative pathway.
- Non-food materials and supplied evidence are always accepted.
- No activity depends exclusively on smell, taste, touch, colour perception, or
  fine motor control.
- Alternative evidence formats assess the same learning outcome without
  penalty.

## Cost and materials

Nothing in the course is priced as a barrier to completing it.

- No assessment may require expensive or specialist ingredients.
- Purchasing premium food earns no marks.
- Required physical materials are supplied, or are replaceable with a common
  non-food analogue.
- A test uses the smallest specimen capable of answering the question.

## Waste

Material used is material accounted for.

- A test protocol justifies the amount of material it uses.
- Tested food must not later be represented as safe to consume.
- Non-food models are preferred when repeated destructive testing is required.
- Waste reduction is treated as a design constraint, not as a taste or
  sustainability bonus mark.

## Photography, privacy and consent

Evidence records a specimen, not a person.

- You may photograph your own specimens or any supplied case.
- Identifiable people and private domestic spaces require consent.
- No assessment requires photographing workers or customers without permission.
- Supplied evidence remains available whenever field photography is
  impractical.

## Cultural and dietary respect

The course does not hold a single correct definition of a sandwich, and does not
ask you to adopt one.

- No universal or culturally neutral definition of a sandwich is enforced.
- A classification argument states its criteria rather than dismissing an
  unfamiliar food form. This is the work of
  [Week 1: What Counts as a Sandwich?](/sessions/week-01/).
- Dietary practice is never treated as a structural defect.
- You are assessed on reasoning and evidence, not on ingredient choice.

## Assessment fairness

What is marked is the test and the reasoning, not the food.

- Taste, presentation, culinary skill, and consumption are excluded from
  marking. [The assessment overview](/assessments/) sets out what each piece of
  work is worth and how it is judged.
- The physical, analogue, and supplied-evidence pathways are equivalent.
- A specimen marked <span class="slop-stamp slop-stamp--failed">Failed</span>
  may still receive strong marks when the test is well designed, documented,
  and interpreted.
- Fabricated measurements and undocumented claims do not count as evidence.

## Before you begin a test

Each week's test station sets its own conditions, listed in the
[weeks index](/sessions/). These eight checks apply to all of them. Work
through them before you load anything.

- I know which of the three routes I am taking, and I know I do not have to
  explain why.
- I can state the learning outcome this test is evidence for.
- I can state the one variable the test changes, and the observation that will
  record it.
- I am using the smallest specimen that can answer the question.
- Nothing in my protocol requires me, or anyone else, to taste or eat a
  specimen.
- Any cutting uses a pre-cut specimen, a diagram, or supervised equipment, and
  the workspace is clean.
- Any photograph I intend to take contains no identifiable person and no
  private domestic space without consent.
- I know what happens to the material afterwards, and I will not offer tested
  food to anyone.
