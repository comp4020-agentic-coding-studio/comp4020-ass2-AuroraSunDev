Most weeks in SLOP2895 are spent at a bench rather than in a lecture theatre.
Four are not. Two of them open the course, because a method has to be agreed
before it can be used; the other two exist because two ideas here cannot be
arrived at by handling a specimen at all.

**Week 1, [A Sandwich Is a Structure](/lectures/week-01/).** What the course
treats a sandwich as, why that definition has to be argued for rather than
assumed, and what will count as evidence for the rest of the semester.

**Week 2, [Beams, Spans and Recovery](/lectures/week-02/).** The outer layer as
a structural member, and the difference between bending that comes back and
bending that does not — the distinction every later observation rests on.

**Week 4, [Moisture Is a Load](/lectures/moisture-is-a-load/).** A compression
failure happens while you watch it. A moisture failure has already happened by
the time you look, and the specimen that failed is the specimen you tested plus
a delay. The lecture is where the delay gets modelled, so that a bench session
can be designed to catch it.

**Week 8, [The Order of Layers](/lectures/the-order-of-layers/).** Reordering an
assembly changes nothing about its materials and everything about which
interfaces exist. Nothing visibly happens when you rearrange a stack, which is
exactly why the argument has to be made in front of a drawing before anyone
builds one.

The last two carry a real deck you can page through in a browser, and each has
a readable equivalent that needs no presenter — the lecture page for Week 4,
and the [week page](/sessions/week-08/) for Week 8. The transcript and the
slides say the same things; neither is a summary of the other.
