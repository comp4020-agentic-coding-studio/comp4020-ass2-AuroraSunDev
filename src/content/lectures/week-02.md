The first lecture with a measurement in it. It borrows one idea from structural
engineering — the beam — and asks how far the borrowing survives contact with a
porous, inconsistent, changing material.

## The borrowing

A beam under load carries compression along one surface and tension along the
other, and its deflection under a central point load rises with the cube of its
span. Halve the span and the deflection falls by a factor of eight.

That relation is exact for a uniform elastic beam. An outer layer is not one:
its stiffness varies across its own width, and it is losing moisture to the air
while the test runs. The lecture is explicit that the beam is a model being
tried on for fit, not a fact about lunch.

## Elastic and plastic

The distinction that matters for the rest of the semester is between behaviour
that reverses when the load is removed and behaviour that does not. A structure
designed to stay in the first regime has a safety margin. Lunch routinely
operates in the second.

So the Week 2 record is not "how much did it bend" but "at which load step did
the behaviour change, and what came back afterwards".
