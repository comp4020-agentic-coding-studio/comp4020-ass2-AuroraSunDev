**Structural question — how does a cut change support, grip and exposed
boundaries?**

A cut is usually treated as the last step of assembly. Structurally it is a
second design, and it is the one the specimen has to survive.

## What a cut removes

A cut removes continuity. One body with two supports and a single span becomes
two bodies, each with a new free edge, a shorter span, and one edge no longer
confined by the outer layer. Nothing was added and nothing taken away, and the
structure is different.

Span is the first change and the largest. Central deflection under the same
point load falls by a factor of eight when the span is halved, so each half is
markedly stiffer than the whole was — which also means the hand holding it can
apply more force before anything is felt to give. The second change is the
exposed cross-section. Every interface that ran through the assembly now
terminates at an open face, and interfaces that were sealed on all sides become
boundaries with somewhere to go.

Cut angle governs how much of that face there is. A cut at angle θ to the
perpendicular exposes a face of length w / cos θ across a specimen of width w,
so a forty-five degree cut opens roughly 1.4 times the face of a perpendicular
one and leaves a tapering section with least material at the thin end. The
course treats the cut as geometrically clean — no crushing, no drag through the
layers — which is false of any real cut and necessary if geometry is to be
studied by itself.

## Investigation

This station issues no cutting instruction and holds no blades for general use.
You work from pre-cut specimens supplied by the laboratory, from the station's
cut-path diagrams, or with equipment operated by a demonstrator on request. All
three participation routes reach the same drawing board: a pre-cut physical
specimen, a non-food analogue cut to the same geometry, or the supplied pack of
cut-face photographs.

On the board, draw the specimen in section and mark the cut path. Then mark the
supports and span before and after, the interfaces the cut opened, and the force
lines. Measure the exposed face for two cut angles across the same width and
compare.

## Failure condition

The specimen holds while whole and loses a layer through the new free edge under
hand pressure once cut. The cut face, not the fold, is the exit.

## What leaves the session

A **Geometry Analysis**: one section drawing carrying the cut path, the spans and
supports before and after, the opened interfaces, and measured exposed-face
lengths for two angles. The Failure Autopsy reasons backwards from a failed
specimen, and cut geometry sets which sequences were available to it.
