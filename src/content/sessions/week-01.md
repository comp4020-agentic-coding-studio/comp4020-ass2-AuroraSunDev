**Structural question — is the boundary defined by shape, materials, function,
or use?**

The course needs a working definition before it can test anything, and the
definition has to survive contact with awkward specimens. This week builds one,
then attacks it.

## The classification problem

Every criterion you might reach for draws a boundary somewhere inconvenient. A
definition by *enclosure* admits a filled roll and excludes an open-faced
assembly. A definition by *count of outer layers* admits a triple-decker and
excludes a wrap. A definition by *use* — held in the hand, eaten without
cutlery — admits things no other criterion does.

None of these is correct. What matters for the rest of the semester is that
you can say which criterion you are using and what it costs you, because the
criterion determines which failures are even visible. A definition that
excludes the wrap cannot describe how a wrap fails.

## Investigation

Work through a branching classifier against a set of disputed specimens: a
filled roll, an open-faced assembly, a wrap, a two-layer stack cut through the
diagonal, and one specimen of your own choosing. For each, record the criterion
that decides it and the first criterion that would reverse the decision.

Use whichever participation route suits you: a physical specimen, a non-food
analogue, or the supplied evidence pack. All three answer the same question.

## Failure condition

A classification fails here when it cannot be applied by a second person to the
same specimen and reach the same result. Ambiguity is the failure mode, and it
is the one you are looking for.

## What leaves the session

A **Classification Field Note**: one annotated specimen, the criterion applied,
the boundary it draws, and one counterexample that the criterion handles badly.
This is the first entry in the evidence trail the Sandwich Field Log assesses.
