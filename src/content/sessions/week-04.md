**Structural question — how does liquid progressively compromise a boundary?**

Liquid does not soak a layer evenly. It advances as a front with a position,
and the position can be marked at a stated time. This week marks it three
times.

## The advancing front

Capillary action drives the front: surface tension at the pore walls draws
liquid inward, and viscosity resists. For idealised uniform pores the distance
advanced grows with the square root of elapsed time rather than in proportion
to it, so a front that crosses half a layer in five minutes does not cross the
remaining half in the next five. Real layers have irregular pores and the
relationship is rougher than the idealisation, but the deceleration is
dependable enough to schedule an observation around.

Pore size sets the trade. Fine pores pull harder and flow more slowly — high
suction, low rate. Coarse pores admit liquid quickly and give it up just as
readily. Two layers can reach the same wetted fraction by opposite routes, and
the route is what your record needs to show.

The load in the week's title is not the weight of the liquid. A few grams
change nothing the beam reasoning of Week 2 would notice. What changes is
stiffness: the wetted region stops carrying tension, and the layer's effective
span shortens to whatever is still dry. Separation then tends to occur at the
boundary between wet and dry material, where stiffness changes sharply over a
short distance, rather than at the wettest point. That is the counter-intuitive
result the week exists to make observable, and it is also the argument of the
course's lecture deck, *Moisture Is a Load*, which should be read before the
laboratory rather than after it.

## Investigation

In the translucent moisture laboratory, introduce liquid at one face of a
supported specimen and mark the front position on the acetate overlay at
`t=0`, `t=5` and `t=15` minutes. Hold one dry specimen alongside as a control.
At each mark, load the specimen briefly and note deflection against the dry
case.

Three routes produce the same overlay: a physical specimen, a porous non-food
analogue of comparable thickness, or the supplied timed photograph set traced
by hand. The evidence is the sequence of front positions, not the liquid.

## Failure condition

A hinge, tear or separation forms along the wet–dry boundary while the far face
is still dry. Record the elapsed time and the front position at the moment it
appeared.

## What leaves the session

An **Absorption Observation**: one overlay carrying three dated front
positions, the interval between them, the dry control, and a note of where the
specimen failed relative to the front. Moisture placement is one of the
variables the Component Test accepts as a declared variable, and this overlay
is the form its evidence takes.
