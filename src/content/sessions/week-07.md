**Structural question — how does temperature change one assembly's behaviour
over time?**

A warm assembly and a cool one are not two designs. They are the same design at
two points on a curve. This week measures the curve.

## The cooling boundary

Heat moves from the warmer region to the cooler one until the difference is
gone. An assembly that leaves the bench warm therefore carries a gradient —
warmest at the interior layer that supplied the heat, coolest at the outer
surface losing it to the room. There is no single temperature to record, only a
temperature and the place it was taken.

The gradient matters because it moves water. Vapour driven out of a warm layer
travels outwards, meets a surface below its dew point — usually the inner face
of the outer layer, or the inside of a wrapper — and condenses there as liquid.
This is moisture arriving at the boundary from *inside* the assembly, which is a
different arrival from the migration Week 4 records, and it is invisible from
outside.

Time then does the rest. The gradient collapses within minutes; the liquid it
deposited does not leave. A specimen measured at equilibrium reads as
unremarkable while carrying the whole thermal history of the twenty minutes
before it. The course treats the assembly as three lumped thermal zones rather
than a continuous field — a teaching simplification, not a claim about how heat
actually distributes.

## Investigation

At the thermal timeline station, one specimen condition is observed at three
fixed time points: on assembly, at ten minutes, and at twenty. Nothing is
observed between them. At each point, record a temperature and the location it
was taken from, the condition of the inner face of the outer layer, and where
liquid is visible.

Run an ambient control alongside. Without it you cannot separate what
temperature did from what twenty minutes would have done anyway.

## Failure condition

The inner face of the outer layer is wet while its outer face is still dry. The
boundary has been loaded from inside, and the specimen looks intact until it is
opened.

## What leaves the session

A **Thermal Comparison**: three timed observations of one condition against an
ambient control, each with its measurement location, and a statement of which
change you attribute to temperature rather than to elapsed time. The Failure
Autopsy reasons from sequences, and this is the first artefact that hands it one
with times on it.
