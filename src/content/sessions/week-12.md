**Structural question — can a design remain coherent under a declared use
condition?**

Nothing holds in general. A structure holds against a stated condition, and the
statement comes first.

## Designing to a declared load case

A **load case** is a written description of the condition a system is required
to survive: transport in a pack for forty minutes, one-handed use, delayed
service, vertical storage, repeated handling. It names the loads, their
duration and the order they arrive in. Until it is written down, "does it hold"
has no answer, because the same assembly passes and fails depending on which
unstated condition the reader had in mind.

Declaring the case narrows the design and makes it assessable. A system built
for vertical storage may reasonably accept behaviour that would count as
failure under one-handed use, and that trade is defensible precisely because
the case is on the page. A system built for every condition has been tested
against none.

The defence is the other half of the work. A structural decision is defended by
showing the declared case, the test that applied it, the point at which the
system first failed, the revision that followed, and the evidence that the
revision addressed the failure rather than displaced it somewhere less visible.
A documented failure with a justified revision carries more weight here than an
untested success — that is a course-design position about what counts as
evidence, not a claim about how materials behave.

## Investigation

At the final rig, declare the load case in writing before testing: loads,
durations, sequence. Then test the system against that case and only that case.
Measure to the condition you declared. Observe where the assembly first departs
from its intended geometry, and record the load state at that moment rather
than the end state alone.

Revise once, then test again under the same declared case. At the defence
bench, defend the result: present the case, the first failure, the revision and
its justification, and compare the two runs directly. Where the second run
still fails, say which part of the case it fails under.

## Failure condition

The declared load case is not met — the system does not survive the condition
it was designed for. This does not undo the work. A failure produced under a
declared case, measured, documented and honestly reported is strong evidence;
an unexamined success is not evidence at all.

## What leaves the session

A **Defended Final System**: the written load case, the annotated assembly with
its declared constraints, the test protocol, two runs of evidence, one
documented failure, one justified revision, and the structural defence that
connects them. This is the submission The Sandwich Must Hold assesses, and it
is what the semester leaves behind — the evidence outlasts the specimen.
