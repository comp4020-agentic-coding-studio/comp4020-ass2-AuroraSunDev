**Structural question — why do individually stable materials move once
assembled?**

A layer that sits still on the bench begins to travel the moment it is placed
between two others. The movement is not a property of the layer. It is a
property of the interface.

## Friction, normal force and the angle of first movement

Resistance to sliding is not a fixed quantity a material carries around with
it. In the standard dry-friction model, the force available to stop two
surfaces sliding is proportional to the force pressing them together, and the
constant of proportionality belongs to the *pair* of surfaces rather than to
either one alone. The question is therefore never "is this material slippery"
but "slippery against what, and under how much load".

Tilt the pair and that relationship becomes something you can read off an
instrument. As the angle increases, the component of weight acting along the
interface grows while the component pressing the surfaces together shrinks.
Slip begins at the angle where the first exceeds what the second can hold —
and the resistance drops as soon as motion starts, which is why the first
movement is usually the whole movement.

Treat the model as an approximation and say so in your record. It assumes
rigid, dry, unchanging surfaces, and the materials on this bench are none of
those: they deform under the load, they exchange moisture across the boundary,
and the coefficient you infer at the start of a session is not the one
operating twenty minutes later. The model earns its place because it makes a
prediction you can check, not because it is true.

## Investigation

On the adjustable slippage ramp, assemble a two-layer pair and raise the ramp
until the upper layer first moves. Record the angle. Repeat the same upper
layer against three different lower surfaces, then repeat one pairing with
additional normal load applied through the clamp plate.

Compare across interfaces, not across specimens. The reading that matters is
the difference between pairings, and whether added normal force moved the
threshold in the direction the model predicts. The ramp accepts a physical
specimen, a non-food analogue cut to the same footprint, or the supplied
displacement series, and returns an angle in all three cases.

## Failure condition

The upper layer travels far enough along the ruled displacement line to leave
the footprint of the layer beneath it. Record the angle at which motion began,
not the angle at which you noticed it.

## What leaves the session

An **Interface Comparison**: three pairings, the angle of first movement for
each, the normal load applied, and one line on which interface you would trust
under hand pressure and why. This is the first evidence in the shape the
Component Test asks for — one variable, held steady, tested under more than one
condition.
