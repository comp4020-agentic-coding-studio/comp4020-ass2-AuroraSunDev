**Structural question — how can a failure sequence be reconstructed from
limited evidence?**

This is the destructive-testing week. The specimens arrive already failed, and
nothing about how they failed can be recovered by asking.

## Reconstruction from incomplete evidence

A failed assembly presents an end state. What it does not present is an order.
A displaced upper layer, a wetted lower boundary and a torn edge are three
observations; whether the wetting preceded the displacement or followed it is
not an observation but a claim, and a claim needs an argument.

The discipline is separating **correlation of appearance** from **established
cause**. Two specimens that look alike at the end may have arrived by different
routes, and a failure that resembles the Week 4 moisture cases is not thereby a
moisture failure. Resemblance narrows the candidate set; it does not close it.

So competing explanations are held open together. Each must account for every
fragment in the inventory, or state which fragment it cannot account for, and
the explanation that survives is the one leaving fewest fragments unexplained.
That ranking is a course convention for making reasoning visible rather than a
measure of real-world likelihood — a rare cause can still be the true one, and
the method is designed to record that rather than resolve it.

An honest reconstruction therefore ends with something unresolved. A dossier
containing no unresolved uncertainty is not a stronger diagnosis; it is a
diagnostic sign in itself.

## Investigation

In the failure-autopsy room, take an evidence set you did not produce:
fragments, a photograph or two, a partial record of the conditions. Inventory
it before proposing anything — every fragment listed and marked observed,
inferred or unknown.

Then build the causal chain as a branch rather than a line. At each point where
the evidence admits two orders, record both and state what evidence would
decide between them. Diagnose the sequence you consider most defensible, name
at least one competing explanation you cannot eliminate, and say what you would
have needed in order to eliminate it.

## Failure condition

A reconstruction fails when a resemblance has been written up as a cause: an
assembly recorded as having failed by slippage because it looks like the
slippage cases, with no evidence of the interface condition at the time. The
tell is a chain with no branches and no unknowns.

## What leaves the session

A **Failure Autopsy**: an evidence inventory with observed, inferred and
unknown separated; a branching causal chain with the decisive evidence named at
each branch; one competing explanation left standing; and a stated residue of
uncertainty. This is the working core of the Failure Autopsy assessment, which
marks the treatment of that uncertainty alongside the reconstruction itself.
