**Structural question — does packaging protect the assembly or impose a new
load?**

Packaging is usually treated as what happens after the structure is finished.
It is a structural member, and it is loaded.

## Packaging as a loaded member

A wrapper, a box or a film does three things at once, and the three are not
compatible. It can **brace**, restraining layers laterally so they cannot
travel — the behaviour Week 3 measured as slippage. It can **compress**: a film
drawn tight applies a continuous inward load the assembly never carried while
it sat on the bench. And it can **enclose**, converting a boundary that was
drying into one that is not, which is the mechanism Week 4 treats as a load in
its own right.

Transport then adds what a static bench cannot. Vibration, tilt held for
minutes rather than seconds, and repeated handling arrive in sequence, and a
specimen that survives each one alone may not survive the order. Treating the
three as separable inputs is a course-design simplification rather than a
description of a real commute; they interact, and we separate them only because
a test that holds one variable still is worth more than a test that reproduces
a journey exactly.

The question to hold in front of you is which function your packaging is
actually performing, and what that function costs. Bracing bought by
compression is bracing paid for in crushed layers.

## Investigation

At the packaging and transport dossier, first classify the packaging on one
specimen by the function it performs first: brace, compress or enclose. Then
run the route.

A route trace is three stations in fixed order — a tilt held at a recorded
angle, a compression load applied through the packaging rather than directly to
the layer, and a handling sequence of a recorded number of pick-ups. Photograph
the specimen at each station twice, in its packaging and then out of it.
Compare the unpackaged condition against the Week 2 or Week 9 record of the
same specimen, and diagnose whether the packaging moved the point of first
failure or merely hid it.

## Failure condition

The packaging holds and the assembly does not — outer geometry preserved while
the layers inside have displaced, compressed or wetted. Record the station at
which this first became visible, and note that it is visible only on opening. A
dossier with no unpackaged photograph cannot show it at all.

## What leaves the session

A **Transport Test**: the classified packaging function, the route trace with
recorded tilt angle, load and handling count, paired packaged and unpackaged
photographs at each station, and a statement of where first failure moved to.
It hands the Failure Autopsy a case whose history is documented rather than
reconstructed after the fact.
