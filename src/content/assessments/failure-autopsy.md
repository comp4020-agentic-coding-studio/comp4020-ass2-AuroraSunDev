**Weight 25% · Capability: Diagnose · Due 14 May 2027, end of Week 11**

> Reconstruct the most defensible failure sequence one supplied case will
> support, and say clearly where the evidence stops.

## Task at a glance

- **Mode** — individual.
- **Format** — a forensic report built on one case from the supplied
  failure-case pack: evidence inventory, event sequence, annotated failure
  diagram, causal chain, competing explanation, uncertainty record.
- **Scale** — one case, worked to the limit of its evidence rather than
  several cases surveyed.
- **Evidence** — the supplied pack. You may not substitute a specimen of your
  own for the case, though you may reference your own earlier tests as
  comparison material.
- **Route** — one file, submitted to the SLOP2895 submission point before the
  deadline.

## The brief

Each case in the pack is a structure that has already come apart, documented
after the fact by someone who was not watching when it happened. You get
photographs taken from two or three angles, a handful of measurements, some
context about handling and transport, and gaps. The gaps are not an oversight in
the pack. They are the condition every real failure investigation works under,
and they are the reason this task exists.

The declared problem is that a failure's appearance and a failure's cause are
different things, and the first is much easier to obtain than the second. A
separated interface looks the same whether it separated because the interface
layer lost grip, because the layer above it was displaced by a shear load during
transport, or because moisture had already compromised the boundary hours
earlier and the handling merely finished it. Visual resemblance to a failure
mode you have seen before is a hypothesis. It is never, on its own, a confirmed
cause, and a report that treats it as one loses marks under evidence handling
however confident it sounds.

What is assessed is the reconstruction: an ordered sequence of events in which
each step is supported by a named item of evidence, at least one rival sequence
that the same evidence also permits, and a clear account of what would
distinguish them if you could go back and measure it. A report that reaches a
confident single answer the evidence cannot support marks below one that reaches
two candidate sequences and says precisely why it cannot choose.

## Required submission

1. An evidence inventory: every item in the case, numbered, with what it shows
   and what it does not, and an explicit list of the evidence the case lacks.
2. A proposed event sequence, ordered in time, with each step keyed to the
   inventory items that support it.
3. An annotated failure diagram locating the initiation point, the propagation
   path and the final separation on the specimen itself.
4. A probable causal chain, distinguishing the initiating condition, the
   contributing conditions and the immediate trigger.
5. At least one competing explanation, tested against the same inventory rather
   than dismissed in a sentence.
6. A record of unresolved uncertainty: what remains undetermined, and the
   specific observation or measurement that would settle it.

## Recommended process

1. **Observe** the case in full and inventory it before forming any account of
   what happened.
2. **Document** what each item can and cannot establish, including the angles
   and regions the photographs never show.
3. **Classify** the failure by where it occurred — within a layer, at an
   interface, or across the assembly — before proposing why.
4. **Diagnose** an initiation point, and state the evidence that places it
   there rather than one layer over.
5. **Compare** at least two sequences that the inventory permits, and identify
   the evidence each one predicts.
6. **Test** both sequences against the items that ought to distinguish them,
   and report the result even when it separates nothing.
7. **Revise** the causal chain to the strongest claim the evidence still
   supports after that comparison.
8. **Defend** the surviving reconstruction by naming the single observation
   that would overturn it.

## Evidence standard

This report stands or falls on four registers staying visibly apart.

**Observed** is what the pack shows, cited by inventory number. If a claim
cannot be traced to a numbered item, it is not an observation.

**Inferred** is the step from evidence to event. Each inference states the items
it rests on and the assumption it adds to them — that a mark was made in one
direction, that two items document the same moment, that the specimen was
upright when photographed.

**Uncertain** covers what the evidence underdetermines: sequence where only
outcome is visible, elapsed time, conditions before documentation began, and any
point where two explanations remain equally supported. Name the measurement that
would resolve each one.

**Limitation** is what the pack itself cannot deliver — the angle not
photographed, the interface not sectioned, the handling not recorded — as
distinct from what you chose not to pursue.

An uncertainty closed by assertion is a heavier error here than an uncertainty
left open, because the report's value is the boundary it draws around what is
actually known.

## Equivalent participation

The case comes from the supplied pack for every student, so the evidence base is
identical across the cohort. The three routes apply to any supporting work you
choose to bring alongside it:

- **Physical specimen** — reproduce a candidate failure mode to compare against
  the case, with no requirement to consume anything.
- **Non-food analogue** — reproduce the same mode in foam, sponge, cardboard,
  clay or textile.
- **Supplied evidence** — work from the pack alone, using the comparison cases
  provided with it.

All three assess the same outcome and no route loses marks. Supporting work is
optional in every route; a report resting entirely on the supplied pack can
receive full marks. See [the participation policies](/policies/).

## Safety, cost and waste

The core of this task requires no materials at all. Any comparison specimen you
choose to make must be the smallest that serves the comparison, and a non-food
analogue is preferred wherever the comparison is destructive. Nothing tested may
be presented to anyone as safe to eat. Cases in the pack are cleared for
reproduction within the course; do not republish them elsewhere. Full detail is
on [the policies page](/policies/).
